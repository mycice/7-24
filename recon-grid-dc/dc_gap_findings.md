### C01 [missing] Octree subdivision after the uniform background grid
- **@** p3 Sec 2 ("divided into a uniform background grid, followed by adaptive octree subdivision")
- **Paper says:** The pipeline is explicitly TWO stages: (1) divide volumetric data into a UNIFORM background grid, then (2) apply ADAPTIVE OCTREE subdivision. The intersection properties of each *cube* with the isosurface are then recorded. The DC framework is classically adaptive/octree precisely so cubes near the isosurface are refined and interior/empty cubes stay coarse.
- **Implication:** Our summary (paper2_DC_summary.md lines 10, and the slice in the build order) describes ONLY a uniform hexahedral voxel grid and never mentions octree subdivision at all. If we implement a flat uniform grid we diverge from the paper's stated preprocessing. CRITICAL ambiguity to pin down: the rest of the paper (physics in 2.2, the 8-vertex/12-edge per-voxel graph, the 6-neighbor spring topology, 0.25 cm fixed voxel size, 54,301 particles) all assume a UNIFORM hexahedral grid where each particle has exactly 6 axis-aligned neighbors. An adaptive octree would create T-junctions and varying neighbor counts that are incompatible with the fixed 6-spring/15-bending-pair topology. So either (a) the octree is used only for the static outer-surface preprocessing and the deformable physics grid is uniform, or (b) the word "octree" is loosely used. We must decide explicitly; the paper never reconciles octree subdivision with the uniform 6-neighbor physical grid.

### C02 [partial] Eq 2 vs Eq 3 index range mismatch (sum 1..n) and Eq3 sign/normal-squared form
- **@** Eq 2, Eq 3 (p3)
- **Paper says:** Eq2: x0 = (Σ_{i=1..n} p_i)/n (centroid of n intersection points). Eq3: F = Σ_{i=1..n} ( -(x0 - p_i) · n_i^2 ). Note Eq3 uses n_i^2 (normal squared / dotted) and the sign is -(x0 - p_i) = (p_i - x0). Eq1 QEF indexes i=0..n-1 while Eq2/Eq3 index i=1..n — an off-by-one in the paper's own notation (same n intersection points).
- **Implication:** Our summary (lines 21) writes 'F = Σ -(x0-p_i)*n_i^2' which matches, but the n_i^2 term is ambiguous: it likely means the descent direction is -(x0-p_i) projected/scaled by the normal — i.e. F is the negative QEF gradient Σ 2 n_i (n_i·(x0-p_i)) but the paper writes it as -(x0-p_i)·n_i^2 which is dimensionally a per-edge vector. Implementation must decide whether n_i^2 means (n_i·n_i)=1 (unit normals) making F = Σ(p_i-x0), or the proper QEF gradient Σ n_i(n_i·(p_i-x0)). These give DIFFERENT directions. The paper's loose notation must be resolved against ref [31] (Schmitz 2009 particle-based QEF). This is a correctness-critical ambiguity in the feature-point solver.

### C03 [misread] Eq3 force term '(x0 - p_i)·n_i^2': what 'n_i^2' actually denotes (scalar |n|^2 vs outer-product/projection)
- **@** p.3 Eq(3); ref [31] Schmitz 2009
- **Paper says:** F = sum_{i=1..n} ( -(x0 - p_i)·n_i^2 ). Source method is Schmitz/Schaefer particle-based QEF, where the per-edge force is the projection of the residual onto the normal, re-scaled by the normal: f_i = ((p_i - x0)·n_i) n_i, so F = sum ((p_i - x0)·n_i) n_i. The paper's compact '·n_i^2' is shorthand for (residual·n_i)·n_i (i.e. n_i n_i^T applied to (x0-p_i)), NOT a scalar |n_i|^2 multiply.
- **Implication:** Our summary copies the literal '-(x0 - p_i)·n_i^2'. If implemented as scalar |n_i|^2 (=1 for unit normals), F = sum -(x0 - p_i) = -(x0 - centroid) = 0 at x0=mean (Eq2) -> the very first step is identically zero and the feature point never moves off the average. Must implement F = sum ((p_i - x0)·n_i) n_i (projection onto each normal). This is the single most likely cause of a wrong, non-converging implementation.

### C04 [partial] Sign of the descent direction F and direction of the step x1 = x0 + a·F
- **@** p.3 Eq(3)-(4), Fig 2.2 (gray 'Direction' arrow / F)
- **Paper says:** F = sum -(x0 - p_i)·n_i^2 = sum (p_i - x0)·n_i^2; x1 = x0 + a·F with a>0. Fig 2.2 confirms F points FROM x0 TOWARD the surface (gray arrow up-right), and x1 lands on the green feature point on the level set.
- **Implication:** The (p_i - x0) sign (note the leading minus folds into (p_i - x0)) makes F a descent direction that pulls the point toward the planes through p_i with normal n_i. Getting the sign backwards pushes the feature point away from the surface, outside the voxel, and Eq5 will immediately stop with a garbage point. Pin the sign by the figure: x moves toward the surface.

### C18 [partial] Two conflicting parameterizations of the stored cut point. The paper text states the cut point's local coords are stored 'relative to the two vertices of the edge' (an edge-endpoint barycentric/interpolation parameterization), but the next sentence and Fig 2.7 say the new cut point 'is computed using the corresponding coordinate axes' (a per-particle axis-offset parameterization). The summary collapses these into one ('local coords on the undeformed edge, re-evaluated through current frame'), masking the ambiguity.
- **@** p.4 Sec 2.1.2, lines 363-368; Fig 2.7 (p.5)
- **Paper says:** 'When an edge is cut, the local coordinates of the cut point, relative to the two vertices of the edge, are recorded ... when the cut is complete ... the new cut point is computed using the corresponding coordinate axes.'
- **Implication:** These are NOT equivalent under deformation. Edge-endpoint interpolation places the cut point on the line between the two CURRENT particle positions (follows both particles). Per-particle-axis offset places the cut point as (particle_pos + R*local_offset_along_one_axis), anchored to ONE particle and its rotation only. Fig 2.7 visually shows the latter: the red cut point is pinned to V3's +X (red) axis and swings with V3's frame, independent of the far endpoint. A strict implementation should anchor each cut point to ONE particle's frame (the +/- axis that maps to that edge), reconstructing world pos = x_particle + R_particle * (axis_dir * stored_signed_distance). Choosing the wrong one changes cut-surface geometry under deformation.

### C26 [covered] Two-class, ordered particle-state machine: OUTSIDE-isosurface particles vs INSIDE-isosurface particles are handled DIFFERENTLY and in sequence, not uniformly deleted
- **@** p5-6, Sec 2.1.4, lines 490-499
- **Paper says:** "Initially, the particles outside the isosurface are directly deleted and no longer participate in force calculations or surface generation. The particles initially within the isosurface are designated as 'ablated particles', and the edges connecting these particles are marked as 'ablated edges'. At this point, the particles are deleted, cutting points are generated along the 'ablated edges', and finally, all voxel grids are updated and the surface is redrawn."
- **Implication:** The summary line 29 captures the two paths, but pin this down: OUTSIDE particles are deleted with NO cut points and NO surface contribution (they were already exterior — nothing to seal). ### M22 [partial] Alternating-projection is over CONVEX shapes (capsule & triangle) — guarantees the non-expansion / monotone convergence used as the loop invariant
